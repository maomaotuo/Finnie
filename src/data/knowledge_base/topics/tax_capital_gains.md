# Capital gains and holding periods — U.S. federal tax

A realized capital gain arises when proceeds from selling a capital asset exceed its adjusted tax basis. Basis commonly starts with purchase cost but can require adjustments.

For U.S. federal taxes, the usual distinction is how long the asset was owned before disposal: more than one year produces a long-term gain; one year or less produces a short-term gain. This also classifies capital losses.

Net short-term gains enter the ordinary-income tax system. Net long-term gains may qualify for preferential capital-gains treatment. This classification can be explained without specifying income brackets. The applicable rate depends on the taxpayer's circumstances; do not infer a rate from holding period alone.

The holding-period count ordinarily begins the day following acquisition and includes the disposal date. Special rules can apply, including for inherited or gifted property and certain other assets.

This educational summary covers general federal concepts, not personalized calculations or state and foreign tax rules. Check the IRS source for exceptions and tax-year-specific amounts.
